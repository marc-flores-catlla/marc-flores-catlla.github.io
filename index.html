Light Curve Transformer: classifying irregular astronomical time series

Research internship at Scalai · Supervisor: Vicens Gaitan

A Transformer encoder that classifies light curves into 25 variability classes directly from irregular observations (Δt, magnitude, error), with no interpolation to a regular grid. Because real labelled data are scarce, the model is trained on synthetic templates resampled with the cadences and noise of real telescope observations.

  • Learnable time embedding (linear + sinusoidal components with learned frequencies) and masked mean pooling.
  • Noise model that samples photometric errors conditioned on magnitude and time gap.
  • Mixed-precision training with data-driven input clipping, evaluated on synthetic and labelled real catalogues.

Statistical Modelling of Inter-Event Times for Extreme Event Forecasting

BSc thesis in Mathematics · Supervisor: Víctor Navas · 9.1 / 10

A framework for forecasting rare extreme events from the waiting times between threshold exceedances. Exponential, Weibull and Gamma models are compared, including a Gamma regression whose scale depends on the threshold. Applied to solar radio bursts at 1415 MHz (relevant to GNSS), it rejects the homogeneous Poisson model and estimates a ten-year probability of about 19% (±3.4%) for a burst above 106 SFU.

Modeling Skyrmions and 2π-Skyrmions in Magnetic Tracks for Ternary Logic Gates

BSc thesis in Physics · Supervisor: Carles Navau · 9.2 / 10

Ternary logic built on magnetic textures: no object encodes 0, a skyrmion encodes 1 and a 2π-skyrmion encodes 2. Using micromagnetic simulations of the Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equation in nanotracks driven by spin Hall torque, I designed an OR/MAX gate and a two-stage AND/MIN gate. The AND/MIN gate reproduces the full ternary truth table in about 30 ns.
